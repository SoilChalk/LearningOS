# Source 004: Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise

**Title**: Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise
**Authors**: Rose E. Wang, Ana T. Ribeiro, Carly D. Robinson, Susanna Loeb, Dorottya Demszky
**Organization**: Stanford SCALE Initiative
**Publication**: National Student Support Accelerator, November 2025
**URL**: https://scale.stanford.edu/publications/tutor-copilot-human-ai-approach-scaling-real-time-expertise
**Accessed**: 2026-07-29
**Source Type**: research_publication (RCT study)
**Language**: English

---

## Core Capabilities (Directly Supported)

1. **Expert thinking modeling for real-time assistance**
   - Tutor CoPilot models expert thinking to assist tutors in real time
   - System designed to help tutors, not replace them (human-AI collaboration)

2. **Learning effectiveness evidence (RCT)**
   - Randomized controlled trial with 700+ tutors and 1,000+ students from underserved communities
   - Students with tutors using Tutor CoPilot were 4 percentage points more likely to master math topics (p<0.01)
   - **Highest gains for students of lower-rated tutors**: +9 percentage points
   - Low cost: approximately $20/tutor/year

3. **Pedagogical behavior change**
   - Analysis of 350,000+ messages shows system promotes effective pedagogy
   - Increases use of probing questions
   - Reduces generic praise

4. **Expertise-scaling mechanism**
   - System bridges gaps in tutor skills
   - Lower-rated tutors benefit more (+9 p.p. vs. +4 p.p. average)
   - Demonstrates potential to scale expertise in real-world educational settings

---

## Design Implications

1. **Human-AI collaboration model validated**: Real-world RCT confirms that AI assistance to human tutors produces measurable learning gains, particularly for less experienced tutors

2. **Pedagogical action guidance works**: System successfully increases probing questions and decreases generic praise - confirms specific pedagogical behaviors can be guided

3. **Skill gap bridging is possible**: Larger gains for lower-rated tutors (+9 p.p.) demonstrates AI can help bridge expertise gaps

4. **Cost-effectiveness matters**: $20/tutor/year cited explicitly - suggests economic viability is a design consideration

5. **Message-level analysis enables evaluation**: 350,000+ message corpus allows pedagogical behavior measurement

---

## Limitations and Non-Inferences

1. **Domain-specific**: Study focused on math tutoring in underserved communities - generalization to other subjects/contexts requires caution

2. **Tutor-mediated interaction**: AI assists tutors who then interact with students - not direct AI-student interaction

3. **Implementation details not disclosed**: How "expert thinking" is modeled and delivered to tutors not described in abstract

4. **Rating mechanism unclear**: "lower-rated tutors" mentioned but rating methodology not specified

5. **Mastery definition not provided**: "more likely to master math topics" - criteria for mastery not detailed

6. **Message analysis scope**: What constitutes "probing question" vs "generic praise" not defined here

---

## Affected Decisions

- pedagogical_action_selection: probing questions > generic praise (evidence-based guidance)
- learner_modeling: system benefits lower-skill tutors more (adaptive to tutor capability)
- learning_effectiveness: +4 p.p. average, +9 p.p. for lower-rated tutors (RCT evidence)
- human_ai_collaboration: tutor-mediated model (not direct AI tutoring)
- cost_viability: $20/tutor/year establishes economic benchmark
