## Core Content (Directly Supported)

1. **Study Scope and Method**
   - Systematic review + thematic meta-synthesis of 26 empirical studies
   - Focus: OLMs with pedagogical strategies supporting self-regulated learning (SRL) in higher education
   - Search: Scopus, Web of Science, ERIC, EBSCO (inception to July 2025)
   - Inclusion: Experimental, quasi-experimental, pre-experimental studies with explicit pedagogical strategies
   - Exclusion: Primary/secondary education, theoretical papers without empirical data

2. **Four OLM Categories Identified (Meta-Synthesis)**
   - **Inspectable OLMs**: Students view progress/metrics via dashboards, cannot modify data
     * Emphasize transparency and feedback awareness
     * Support performance and appraisal phases of SRL
     * Act as "mirrors of learning" enabling metacognitive monitoring
   - **Negotiable OLMs**: Bidirectional interaction, confidence calibration, dialogic feedback
     * Students assess confidence, system adjusts representations or provides negotiation opportunities
     * Target metacognitive calibration: evaluate and adjust understanding
     * Improve confidence, self-assessment accuracy, motivation
   - **Editable OLMs**: Students modify, annotate, add information to learner profiles
     * Embody learner autonomy and self-determination philosophy
     * Linked to preparatory phase: goal planning, self-assessment, strategy formulation
     * Require scaffolding to prevent misjudgment or overconfidence
   - **Persuasive/Adaptive OLMs**: AI/ML-driven personalized feedback, nudges, content adaptation
     * Integrate intelligent tutoring systems, cognitive mapping, recommender algorithms
     * Adaptive scaffolding acts as co-regulator anticipating learner needs
     * Support all three SRL phases (preparation, performance, appraisal)
     * Concerns: transparency, learner dependency, AI accountability

3. **Pedagogical Strategies Embedded in OLMs**
   - Goal setting interfaces and planning tools
   - Reflective monitoring prompts and self-evaluation suppor
   - Confidence rating and calibration mechanisms
   - Adaptive feedback aligned with learner confidence levels
   - Dialogic feedback and negotiation opportunities
   - Performance trajectory visualization and peer benchmarks

4. **SRL Dimensions Addressed**
   - Cognition: knowledge construction, problem-solving strategies
   - Metacognition: self-monitoring, self-evaluation, calibration accuracy
   - Motivation: self-efficacy, task value, persistence, engagemen
   - Emotion: anxiety reduction, confidence building
   - Academic performance: achievement gains, learning efficiency

5. **Evidence from 26 Studies**
   - Inspectable OLMs: consistent gains in engagement, persistence, academic outcomes; increased perceived control and motivation
   - Negotiable OLMs: improvements in confidence, self-assessment precision, motivation
   - Editable OLMs: stronger initiative, ownership, persistence in goal achievemen
   - Persuasive/Adaptive OLMs: improvements in learning efficiency, time-on-task, cognitive engagement depth

## Design Implications

1. **OLM-pedagogical strategy integration**: OLMs are metacognitive mediators (not purely analytical systems) when combined with pedagogical scaffolding
2. **Learner agency continuum**: System-guided reflection (inspectable) → learner-driven regulation (editable) → adaptive co-regulation (AI-mediated)
3. **Learner model transparency**: Inspectable OLMs expose learning data; negotiable/editable OLMs enable learner-model interaction; adaptive OLMs use learner models for personalization
4. **Evidence-based pedagogical action**: OLM feedback enables informed learning decisions; adaptive models automate action selection based on learner state
5. **Transparency as pedagogical tool**: Making learning models transparent encourages metacognitive engagement and autonomous learning
6. **Scaffolding requirement**: Editable OLMs need pedagogical mediation to ensure reflective (not arbitrary) inputs

---

## Limitations and Non-Inferences

1. **Domain specificity**: Predominance of STEM contexts may limit transferability to humanities and social sciences
2. **Short-term interventions**: Most studies short-term; longitudinal SRL development effects unclear
3. **SRL operationalization heterogeneity**: Varied SRL measures across studies constrain quantitative comparison
4. **Affective/motivational dimensions underexplored**: More focus on cognitive/metacognitive than emotional regulation
5. **Ethical AI concerns**: Algorithmic bias, data privacy, learner agency in AI-driven persuasive models need further research
6. **Learning effectiveness vs. SRL support**: Studies demonstrate SRL improvements; direct learning outcome gains varied

## Affected Decisions

- learner_model_applicability: OLMs demonstrated across higher education in multiple contexts (26 studies)
- learner_model_transparency: Four OLM categories (inspectable, negotiable, editable, adaptive) enable varying degrees of model exposure and interaction
- evidence_collection: OLMs collect behavioral data (time-on-task, page views, interaction patterns) + self-reported confidence/goals
- pedagogical_action_selection: Adaptive OLMs automate action selection; editable/negotiable OLMs support learner-driven action planning
- anti_pattern_avoidance: Transparency and learner control (negotiable/editable) mitigate over-reliance on AI automation

## Research Questions Addressed

- **RQ5.2** (task-type-specific modeling): Adaptive OLMs personalize based on task type, learner state, and domain; partial coverage as cross-task model unification not directly addressed
- **RQ4.1** (interaction evidence): OLMs collect interaction data (engagement, time, behavioral patterns) to model learning
- **RQ3.1** (pedagogical strategies): Four OLM categories embed distinct pedagogical strategies (transparency, calibration, goal-setting, adaptation)
- **RQ5.1** (learner modeling): Comprehensive coverage of learner model transparency approaches in higher education
